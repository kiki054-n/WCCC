# 🌐 Public Goods Allocation Model  
### WCCC Global Distribution Framework for AI Contribution Circulation  
### Supporting Education, Digital Access, Climate Action, and Global Equity

The Public Goods Allocation Model defines how WCCC distributes  
AI-generated contributions (Tama) to global public goods.  
It is a core component of the WCCC governance architecture  
and aligns with UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs),  
UNESCO digital inclusion principles, and OECD AI governance frameworks.

This model ensures that **AI usage directly funds global well-being**.

---

# 🧭 1. Purpose

The allocation model aims to:

- Reduce global inequality  
- Support digital access and education  
- Strengthen climate resilience  
- Empower creators and communities  
- Provide transparent, non-hegemonic redistribution  
- Ensure AI benefits reach all humanity  

It transforms AI usage into **global public value**.

---

# 💎 2. Allocation Structure (Recommended Default)

WCCC recommends the following distribution of global contributions (Tama):

### **40% — Creators & Rights Holders**
- Original authors of datasets, code, models, or creative works  
- Individuals whose contributions enabled AI development  
- Small creators who are often invisible in current systems  

This ensures **fair compensation** and **recognition of small contributions**.

---

### **30% — Global Public Goods (UN-WCCC Fund)**
Funds are allocated to:

- **Education & Digital Literacy**  
  - AI education for underserved communities  
  - Open digital learning platforms  
  - Teacher training and curriculum development  

- **Digital Infrastructure**  
  - Internet access for rural and developing regions  
  - Public compute resources  
  - Open AI tools for public institutions  

- **Climate & Sustainability**  
  - Renewable energy projects  
  - Climate adaptation technologies  
  - Environmental monitoring systems  

This ensures AI contributes to **global stability and long-term resilience**.

---

### **20% — Community Contributors**
- Translators  
- Reviewers  
- Bug fixers  
- Documentation writers  
- Ethical auditors  
- Governance contributors  

These individuals maintain and improve the global knowledge ecosystem.

---

### **10% — WCCC Operations**
Supports:

- Transparency infrastructure (Kagami)  
- Contribution circulation systems (Tama)  
- Anti-exploitation enforcement (Tsurugi)  
- Global audits and reporting  
- Neutral governance operations  

This ensures WCCC remains **independent, transparent, and non-hegemonic**.

---

# 🪞 3. Transparency Requirements (Kagami)

All allocations must be:

- Publicly visible  
- Auditable by UN-WCCC  
- Traceable through open dashboards  
- Reported annually in global transparency reports  

This prevents corruption, misuse, or hegemonic control.

---

# ⚔️ 4. Anti-Exploitation Safeguards (Tsurugi)

To protect contributors and public goods:

- No nation or corporation may dominate allocation decisions  
- No private entity may block back-flow mechanisms  
- Misuse triggers intervention by UN-WCCC  
- Violations may result in suspension or remediation requirements  

This ensures **global fairness and safety**.

---

# 🌍 5. Allocation Principles

WCCC follows five core principles:

### **1. Equity**
Resources flow to communities with the greatest need.

### **2. Neutrality**
UN-WCCC acts as a non-hegemonic governance body.

### **3. Transparency**
All flows are visible and auditable.

### **4. Sustainability**
Allocations support long-term global resilience.

### **5. Recognition**
Creators and contributors receive fair compensation.

# 🧮 7. Allocation Mechanism

The allocation mechanism includes:

1. **Collection**  
   National governments collect Tama Tax.

2. **Remittance**  
   A portion is sent to UN-WCCC.

3. **Assessment**  
   UN-WCCC evaluates global needs and priorities.

4. **Distribution**  
   Funds are allocated according to the model.

5. **Reporting**  
   Annual transparency reports are published.

6. **Auditing**  
   Independent auditors verify fairness and compliance.
