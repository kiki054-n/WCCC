# 📘 BFOL – Back-Flow Open License (Draft v0.1)
### A New Open License for the AI Era  
### Ensuring Fair Contribution, Transparent Circulation, and Global Equity

The **Back-Flow Open License (BFOL)** is an open license designed for the AI era.  
It ensures that knowledge, creativity, and contributions flow freely,  
while guaranteeing that **a portion of benefits automatically circulates back**  
to creators, communities, and global public goods.

BFOL is part of the WCCC (World Contribution Circulation Council) framework  
and aligns with the Three-Layer Model and the TTT Structure  
(Kagami – Transparency, Tama – Contribution, Tsurugi – Intervention).

---

# 🧭 1. Purpose

BFOL aims to:

- Protect creators from exploitation  
- Ensure fair redistribution of AI-generated value  
- Enable global contribution circulation  
- Support non-hegemonic AI development  
- Provide a transparent and enforceable open license for the AI era  

BFOL is designed for:

- AI models  
- Datasets  
- Code  
- Documentation  
- Creative works  
- Knowledge artifacts  

---

# 🪞 2. Kagami Clause – Transparency

To ensure global transparency:

1. AI systems using BFOL-licensed materials must record usage metrics  
   (e.g., token counts, inference calls, dataset access).  
2. No content inspection is required; only usage metrics are reported.  
3. Usage logs must be auditable by UN-WCCC or equivalent neutral bodies.  
4. Public dashboards may be used to display global contribution flows.

This clause prevents hidden exploitation and supports AI Safety.

---

# 💎 3. Tama Clause – Contribution Back-Flow

A portion of commercial or large-scale AI usage must **flow back**  
to creators and global public goods.

### Required Back-Flow Distribution (recommended default):

- **40% → Original creators / rights holders**  
- **30% → Global public goods (UN-WCCC Fund)**  
- **20% → Community contributors (translation, fixes, improvements)**  
- **10% → WCCC operations (auditing, transparency infrastructure)**  

This ensures that **small contributions are recognized and rewarded**,  
and that AI-generated wealth circulates fairly.

---

# ⚔️ 4. Tsurugi Clause – Anti-Exploitation & Enforcement

To prevent misuse:

1. BFOL prohibits exclusive control or monopolization of BFOL-licensed works.  
2. BFOL prohibits extraction of value without back-flow.  
3. BFOL prohibits using BFOL-licensed materials for unethical, harmful,  
   or exploitative purposes.  
4. UN-WCCC or designated neutral bodies may intervene in cases of violation.  
5. Violations may result in:
   - Suspension of license rights  
   - Required remediation  
   - Public disclosure of misuse  

This clause ensures fairness and protects contributors globally.

---

# 🌐 5. Permissions

BFOL grants the following rights:

- ✔ **Commercial use**  
- ✔ **Modification**  
- ✔ **Distribution**  
- ✔ **Private or public deployment**  
- ✔ **Use in AI training, inference, or fine-tuning**  
- ✔ **Integration into larger systems**  

As long as **Back-Flow obligations are respected**.

---

# 🔒 6. Restrictions

BFOL prohibits:

- ❌ Exclusive ownership or privatization of BFOL-licensed works  
- ❌ Removal of attribution  
- ❌ Blocking back-flow mechanisms  
- ❌ Using BFOL materials to build exploitative AI systems  
- ❌ Training models that intentionally obscure contribution origins  

---

# 🧩 7. Attribution Requirements

All BFOL-licensed works must include:

- Name of original creator(s)  
- Link to the original repository  
- BFOL license notice  
- Contribution history (if available)  

Attribution ensures recognition of small contributions.

---

# 🔧 8. Implementation in AI Systems

AI providers using BFOL materials must:

1. Track usage metrics (token counts, dataset access, inference calls).  
2. Implement back-flow payment or contribution mechanisms.  
3. Provide transparency dashboards or reports.  
4. Allow UN-WCCC auditing when required.  

This makes BFOL compatible with:

- LLMs  
- Diffusion models  
- RL systems  
- Datasets  
- OSS codebases  
- Hybrid AI architectures
